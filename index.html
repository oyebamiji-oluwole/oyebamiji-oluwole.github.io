Recent work

New at the Fourth UK AI Conference

Two posters presented in Nottingham in 2026 with Gebregziabihier Nigusie (Mizan-Tepi University). Both replace the quadratic cost of transformer attention with structured kernel interpolation, and both keep uncertainty in view.

Poster · 2026Time series forecasting

SKIT: Structured Kernel Interpolation Transformer for Time Series Forecasting

Self-attention can be read as a kernel. SKIT approximates that kernel with a small set of inducing points through adaptive SKI attention, so long sequences stay cheap in GPU memory.

  • Tested on 5 short-term and 7 long-term forecasting benchmarks, including Weather, Electricity, Traffic and ETT
  • Lowest RMSE and MAE on short-term series such as Beijing PM2.5
  • Best average long-horizon accuracy, competitive with CATS and TimeMixer up to 720 steps ahead
Poster · 2026Earth observation

Probabilistic Spatiotemporal Modelling with Kernelised Attention

A hybrid 3D-CNN, SKI attention and Gaussian process model that forecasts satellite vegetation (NDVI) from incomplete, noisy observations and says how certain each prediction is.

  • Cuts attention cost from O(L²) to O(LM) with M inducing points
  • Applied to Sentinel-2 NDVI at four flood-affected reservoir sites: Alau, Kakhovka, Bartley and Toddbrook
  • Uncertainty-aware, pixel-wise change maps for early warning of abnormal environmental change
About

Statistician by training, machine learning researcher by trade

I'm a DeepMind Academic Fellow in the School of Computer Science at the University of Birmingham. My research covers the theoretical foundations and multidisciplinary applications of machine learning, data mining and computational statistics.

I focus on probabilistic modelling, numerical optimisation and computational methods for high-dimensional spatio-temporal data, with impact in biology, ecology and the environmental sciences. Recent work includes deep Gaussian processes for uncertainty estimation, spatiotemporal transformers and satellite-based environmental monitoring.

Before Birmingham I was a Senior Statistician in the Data Science Group at HR Wallingford, a Senior Research Associate on the EPSRC Data Science for the Natural Environment project at Lancaster University, and a Research Associate at Newcastle University, where I built surrogate models linking microscale biological processes to macroscale engineering models.

I grew up in a village, and everyday life taught me statistics before any classroom did. My grandmother tasted a spoonful of soup to judge the whole pot, and a sip of palm wine told you about the whole bottle. That is sampling and inference.

From my Royal Statistical Society Future Leaders profile

I'm a Royal Statistical Society Future Leader and an advisor for Deep Learning Indaba 2025. I support programmes that open AI and machine learning to underrepresented groups, and I want to be a mentor and role model for the next generation of researchers, especially in Black communities.
